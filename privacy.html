Skeeto, Privacy Policy

Last updated 20 November 2026

Your timetable, assignments, grades and reminders stay on your phone, and they work without an account. A grade leaves your phone only if you choose to send one in a chat. A Skeeto account is optional. You need one for Spotted (called the Feed until November 2026), Friends and messages, and what it stores is kept on Skeeto's server in Canada. If you have an account, your courses, program and year are used to personalise Spotted, to add you to your course communities, and to show on your profile, and you can't switch this off. Your location stays on your phone unless you switch on sharing it for a friend, and then they see only the campus building you are at or near, the parking lot you are in, or just that you are on campus, or you send your location in a chat, which shows the people in that chat where you were, for an hour. Some processing happens in the United States; the details are below. Skeeto doesn't sell your data, doesn't show ads, and doesn't track you across other apps or websites.

Skeeto is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by the University of Toronto. It is an independent app. When you ask it to, it signs in to U of T's own services (ACORN and Quercus) using your own UTORid, on U of T's own sign-in page. Until September 2026 it was called Oakhall. It is the same app from the same developer.

1. Who we are

Skeeto is made and run by Lucas Nadon, an individual developer in Canada (referred to below as "Skeeto", "we" or "the developer"). He is responsible for the personal information Skeeto handles, and he is the person to contact about it, at lucasnadon3132@gmail.com.

This policy covers the Skeeto app for iPhone and for Android, and the server it uses. It is written to meet Canada's federal privacy law (PIPEDA) and, for people in Quebec, Quebec's private-sector privacy law (as amended by Law 25).

2. What Skeeto collects

If you never make an account, Skeeto's server stores nothing about you, apart from short-term technical logs. In that case, besides your own sign-in to U of T, only these things leave your phone. Anonymous usage analytics and crash reports, which you can turn off (see Usage analytics). Requests for public information, such as campus map and food data, term dates and the oldest version of the app that still works, from Skeeto's server, campus photos from Concept3D (the company behind U of T's online map), and U of T's public course timetable. Map images, which come from Apple on iPhone and from Google on Android (see Location). And on Android, the address for notifications that Google's Firebase gives the app when it starts (see Message notifications). None of these include anything about you.

If you do make an account, this is everything Skeeto's server stores about you.

Your account

  • How you sign in. On iPhone, sign in with Apple, Google, or a six-digit code sent to your email. On Android, sign in with a six-digit code sent to your email, or with Google where the app offers it. We store your email address (with Sign in with Apple this may be a private relay address) and the ID Apple or Google uses for you. If Apple or Google gives us your name, we store it as a suggestion for your display name, and so that people at your campus can find you by typing the start of it in Friends → Add or Search. That name is only matched, never shown: results show your username and display name. It is never matched for someone you've blocked, someone who blocked you, or anyone at another campus.
  • A record that you accepted the posting terms, with the version and the time.

Your profile

  • Your username, display name, bio, social handles (Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, Discord) if you add them, a profile photo if you have one, your campus, and the date you joined.
  • Your program of study and year. These are filled in automatically from ACORN (see the next section).
  • How many people follow you (your Broskeetos), how many you follow, and how many posts you have.

Your timetable, courses, program and year

  • Your enrolment: this term's course codes and sections, your program and your year, as read from ACORN. It is uploaded only if you have an account. A new import replaces it. Skeeto's server also uses it to suggest people you may know; a suggestion shows only how many classes you share, never which.
  • Your course communities. You are added automatically to one community per course you take (for example MAT102H5). Your membership is a record on the server.
  • Your week, for Friends. While you're signed in, your current term's timetable is uploaded whenever it is saved on your phone (an import from ACORN, or a class time updated from U of T's public course timetable) and when you open the Friends tab. It is uploaded in two copies. One has times only (when you're busy). The other has the full details: course codes, sections, rooms and times. Both carry the term and the date your timetable was last read from ACORN, so friends can see when it is from an earlier term. Who can read each copy is explained below.
  • Exam times, for the threads after an exam. With an account, when your phone has read UTM's exam schedule, it sends Skeeto's server the schedule's date, start and end time of every sitting of each of your courses this term, every section's, not only yours (for example CSC108H5, 14 December, 9am to 12pm). No section, room or surname split is sent. These are public facts from UTM's page, kept in one shared table with no record of which account sent them or when, and they are only accepted for courses in your enrolment.
  • Sections you watch. Watching a full section in Schedule › Watch needs an account. The sections you watch (course code, term and section, at most ten) and whether "Tell me when a seat opens" is on are saved to your account, so Skeeto's server can check U of T's public course timetable for them while the app is closed and send you a notification when a seat or a waitlist opens. Nobody else can see which sections you watch. The server's requests to U of T carry only the course code, never anything about you, and it asks once per course however many people watch it. Removing a section, signing out, Erase this phone's data, and deleting your account remove them from the server. Your account keeps one list: if another phone signed in to it watches sections, it saves its own list again the next time it opens Skeeto.

Posts, comments, messages and photos

  • Posts: text (up to 2,000 characters), up to four photos, an optional link, and the community you posted in.
  • Comments and replies, which can include one image.
  • Anonymous communities. A community is anonymous or not from the day it is made, and that never changes. In an anonymous community every post and reply is anonymous: others see "Anonymous" on a post, or a two-word name that belongs to you in that one thread (such as "Quiet Heron") on a reply, and never your name, handle, photo, program or year. Everywhere else, posts and replies carry your name. Two kinds of post elsewhere are anonymous too: ones made before November 2026, and the threads Skeeto opens after an exam in a course community. On those you can reply anonymously (the switch starts on), and the person who wrote the post is always anonymous in their own thread. Skeeto's server still records that you wrote it, because reports, strikes, bans, blocks, deleting your own post, removal from a community and the support lines' review (section 5) all need it; only the developer can see that. Anonymous posts and replies can't carry photos or tag anyone, and in an anonymous community they can't contain phone numbers, email addresses or @handles: one that does is refused before it is saved.
  • Community rules you accept. Before your first post or reply in an anonymous community you accept its rules (Skeeto's own, plus any its creator added). Skeeto records which version you accepted and when, and asks again if the rules change.
  • When a post goes out. A post in a community is held and goes out with the others at 10pm, Toronto time; until then only you can see it. Posts to Friends and all replies go out at once.
  • Likes, reposts and stickers, and posts you mark "Not interested".
  • Communities you create: name, description, icon, whether it is anonymous, and any rules you add.
  • Messages you send to and receive from people you're connected with, one to one or in a group chat, when each one was sent, edited or deleted, and when each member last read the conversation (this is what shows "Seen"). For a group chat we also store its name, who is in it, who added them and who runs it. A message can include one photo, or be a card: a post you shared (only which post; each person sees it only if they could see it anyway), your location (deleted from the message after an hour), a grade you chose (the course, "Current grade" and the score, which are the message's words), or a plan to meet or a food run (a time, a place, and who said they're in). Everyone in a group chat can see its members and messages, except that people who have blocked each other don't see each other's messages.
  • Photos. Skeeto uses only the photos you choose or take for a post, comment, message, sticker, community icon or profile photo. A profile photo or a photo in a message, like every other image, is checked by the automated image moderation described below before anyone else can see it; until then only you see it (for a message, the whole message, its words included). It never reads the rest of your library. Each photo is resized and re-saved as a JPEG on your phone before it is uploaded.
  • Reports you make: what you reported and the reason you gave.
  • Food corrections you send ("Something wrong?" on Food): the reason you picked, any words you added (up to 500 characters), which place (or the Food list as a whole), what the screen showed for it and when its hours were last checked, linked to your account. Never where you are. A correction changes nothing until the developer checks it.

Message notifications

  • Your phone's push token. If you allow notifications, the app sends Skeeto's server the push token your phone's notification service gives Skeeto (Apple's on iPhone, Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android): an address for notifications, which says nothing else about you or the phone. It is linked to your account, with whether it is an iPhone test build, the App Store build or an Android phone, and when it was last registered. It is used only to deliver the notifications described here. Signing out on that phone removes it. On Android, Firebase gets that address from Google when the app starts, even before you sign in or allow notifications; Skeeto's server only receives it once you are signed in and have allowed them.
  • Chats you muted: which conversations you don't want notifications from. Nobody else can see this, and the other people in the chat aren't told.
  • What a notification contains. When someone messages you, Skeeto's server sends the notification for your phone to Apple's push notification service (iPhone) or Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android): the sender's display name (and the group's name, in a group chat), the start of the message (up to about 120 characters, or "Sent a photo" for a photo, or "Shared a post", "Shared a location" or "Shared a grade" for those cards, never the grade or the place), how many unread messages you have, and the conversation's ID so a tap opens it. Apple or Google delivers it to your phone. On Android it also carries which kind of notification it is, so your phone can sort it into the right group (Messages, Seat alerts and so on). Nothing is sent for a chat you muted, or from someone either of you has blocked.
  • Spotted notifications. When someone replies to your post (at most once an hour for each post), the notification has their display name, or their two-word name for that thread if they replied anonymously, never who they are, and the start of the reply. At 10pm, "Tonight's drop" says how many new posts went out. It comes only if you use Spotted regularly (see below): at three posts or more, and at one or more for the people who use it most, with how many were in the community they use most (never named if that community is anonymous). If you use Spotted most and your post is among the top three in its community after a drop, one notification says so ("Your post is taking off"), at most once a day; it never quotes the post, and for an anonymous post it doesn't name the community. Switch these off in Account. They only come to a version of Skeeto that has that switch.
  • Exam threads. Five minutes after a course's exam ends, if at least five Skeeto users take the course this term, a thread opens in that course's community ("CSC108H5 exam just ended. How was it?") and members who use Spotted, or who have posted in that course's community this term, get one notification about it. The same switch as Tonight's drop turns these off. Threads close to new replies after 48 hours.
  • A friend's new timetable. When a friend refreshes their timetable for a new term and yours is still from the term before, Skeeto's server sends your phone one notification for that term with their first name ("Maya updated their winter timetable"). It is sent at most once a term, and not if you turned off the Free together switch, if you have blocked each other, or if their week is hidden.
  • Seat alerts. When a section you watch goes from full to having a seat, or its waitlist opens, Skeeto's server sends your phone a notification naming the course and section and when it checked ("A seat opened in CSC108H5 LEC0101"). At most one an hour for each section. Turn "Tell me when a seat opens" off in Schedule › Watch to stop them. It is a head start, not a guarantee: enrolling happens on ACORN.

Your connections

  • Who you follow, and who follows you.
  • Friend requests, connections, and who you have promoted to friend.
  • Your invite link's code: a random code that stands for you, with when it was made and when it expires (two weeks later), was replaced by a new one, or stopped working because you removed or declined someone. Which link someone used isn't recorded. For a friendship an invite made, it is noted that it came from an invite, so that turning location on for everyone leaves that friend out until you tick them; the note goes when the friendship does.
  • A count of the friend requests you sent, so nobody can send more than 30 a day. Each entry is kept until you send a request more than a day later, and is deleted with your account.
  • The communities you have joined, including course communities.
  • People you have blocked.
  • People you may know. Skeeto may suggest people at your campus you may know, worked out on its server from classes you both take this term (or next term, from 30 days before it starts), friends you have in common (counted, never named), the same program or year, communities you are both in, and follows. Your timetable is read for this and never shown: a suggestion shows the person's name, username, photo and one reason, such as "2 classes in common". Suggestions never use your location, your contacts, or anonymous communities. Skeeto stores the people you dismissed with the x, so they aren't suggested again, and whether you allow being suggested: switch it off in Account → Privacy → Suggest me to people I may know. Nobody is suggested while their account is less than a day old, while they are muted or banned, or for 30 days after a strike. People can still find you by searching. Whether that switch is on can be read by anyone who can see your profile.

What you do in Spotted

To decide what to show you, Skeeto records what happens in Spotted, and links it to your account:

  • which posts were shown to you, where in the list they were, and what score the ranking gave them;
  • how long you spent on each post, and whether you opened the full post or its comments;
  • what you liked or unliked, commented on, reposted or un-reposted, shared, or marked "Not interested", and whose profiles you opened from a post;
  • where in the app each of these happened (Spotted, a community, a profile, search).

How much you use Spotted. Each night Skeeto's server works out, from that activity and what you posted and replied in the last 14 and 30 days, how much you use Spotted (one of four levels, from not using it to using it most) and which of your communities you use most (never the Friends community). It keeps the counts behind this: the days you were active and the things you did in the last 14 days, and your posts and replies in the last 30. If you haven't accepted the posting terms, you are at the lowest level. This decides whether Today shows a Spotted post, which one, and which Spotted notifications you get. It is worked out automatically, with no person involved. There is no switch for the level itself; turning off Tonight's drop in Account stops the notifications it decides. Nobody else can see it: your phone is told it only to choose the line on Today's card, and the app doesn't show it. It is replaced each night, and it is deleted with your account.

Your own numbers. Today's Spotted card can show one line about your own posts this week: where your best post ranked in its community, how many likes and replies your posts got, or how many people saw them. Only you see it. It is counts only, never who, and it never quotes a post.

Spotted search. What you type into search is sent to Skeeto's server so it can find matching posts and communities. It isn't stored or logged. It is used only to answer that search.

You can turn it off at Account → Privacy → Help improve Skeeto. Turning it off stops it immediately: nothing more is sent from that moment, including anything waiting to be sent.

Technical data

Like any online service, Skeeto's server providers keep short-term technical logs of the requests your phone makes, such as the IP address, the time and the kind of request. They are used for security and to fix problems. Skeeto's own server code logs IDs and outcomes, not the text or images you post.

Location

Your location is used on your phone, and only if you allow it:

  • to show where you are on the campus map;
  • to estimate how long the walk to your next class will take;
  • for walking directions you start yourself. While a walk is running, the app follows your location in the background, including when the screen is locked, so the directions on your Lock Screen stay up to date. iOS shows its location indicator the whole time. Following stops when you arrive or end the walk. On Android there is no Lock Screen card: a walk follows your location only while the walk screen is open.

None of that is sent anywhere: not to Skeeto's server, not to analytics, not to other students. There are two exceptions, and each happens only when you choose it: sharing where you are with friends, and sending your location in a chat.

Sharing where you are with friends is off until you switch it on for a friend, on their card or in Account → Location; a new friend never gets it on their own. It only works if you allow Skeeto to use your location, and on iPhone only with Precise Location on (on Android, see below). On iPhone, switching it on also asks iOS to allow location "Always", once per phone, so friends can see your place update while Skeeto is closed. You can keep "While Using" instead: sharing still works, but only while Skeeto is open. Android never asks for "Always". When it is on:

  • A place, never a position. Your phone works out which campus building you are at (in it, or within about 40 metres of its walls), or near (within about 150 metres), or which campus parking lot you are in, and sends only that place and which of the three it is ("at", "near" or "in"), or just "on campus" when you are farther from all of them. Your exact position, its accuracy, your distance from the building and your path never leave your phone. On iPhone, if Precise Location is off for Skeeto, nothing is shared at all. On Android, Skeeto never sends a reading less accurate than about 100 metres, so with "Approximate" nothing is shared.
  • Only on campus. Your phone sends a place only while you are on your own campus: Mississauga, St. George or Scarborough. St. George is spread through downtown streets, so there it sends only while you are close to a university building. When you leave, it deletes what it sent. With Skeeto closed, that happens only on an iPhone that allows location "Always"; otherwise, and always on Android, it disappears within 30 minutes. Only friends at your own campus can see it.
  • When it updates. While the app is on your screen, when you move to another place (a building, from at it to near it, or into a lot; only once two readings or a minute agree, so stepping out of a door doesn't change it) and every few minutes. On Android that is all: nothing is read or sent while Skeeto is closed, and a friend looking for you doesn't wake your phone. On an iPhone that allows location "Always", it also updates with Skeeto closed, only on a few events: when you arrive on campus or leave it, when iOS notices you have arrived somewhere or left (a "visit"), when you have moved about 100 metres from the last check, and when a friend you picked looks for you (at most once every two minutes). For each, Skeeto is woken for a moment and takes one reading (and a second a few seconds later, if the first puts you somewhere different), then sends the place, still only on campus. Leaving campus deletes it at once, with no reading (if your phone has no connection then, at the next check or when you next open Skeeto). Your phone watches for these events itself: Skeeto never tracks your location continuously, and between events nothing is read. Visits and moving are watched only on or near your campus: within a circle round it that reaches about 1.2 km from the middle of Mississauga, 1.4 km at Scarborough and 2.2 km at St. George (much of downtown). So a check can happen near campus as well as on it, but one taken off campus is never sent; it only deletes what was there. Farther away, only coming back is watched. The point the 100-metre circle is drawn round stays on your phone, and is forgotten when sharing stops or you sign out. Without "Always", nothing is sent while Skeeto is closed. With "Always" but sharing off (or no friend picked, or signed out), your phone watches for nothing and nothing is read. You can change "Always" back to "While Using" in your iPhone's Settings, under Skeeto → Location.
  • What is stored: one current place (a building's or a parking lot's name and code, and whether you are at, near or in it, or "on campus"), your campus and the time, linked to your account on Skeeto's server. Each new one replaces the last; no history of where you have been is kept.
  • Who sees it: only the friends you switched it on for, as "at IB · now", "near IB · now" or "in P8 · now" on your face on their Friends tab, a line on your card, and your face on that building or lot on the campus map (beside it, never at where you are). One you have also promoted to friend, who already sees your week in full, sees the two side by side, like "in MAT102 · IB" while your timetable has you in a class (or "in MAT102 · near IB" when you are only near it); their phone puts together what it already had, and nothing more is sent. A connection you switched it on for sees the place only. It stops at once if you switch it off for them, or if either of you removes or blocks the other, and becoming friends again doesn't turn it back on.
  • How long: nothing older than 30 minutes is ever shown, and older ones are deleted automatically within five minutes. Turning sharing off, leaving campus (with the app open, or closed with "Always" allowed), signing out or deleting your account deletes it straight away.

Like everything on the server, the developer can technically see a stored place. He doesn't look at them, and they are never used for analytics, Spotted or anything but showing your friends where you are.

Sending your location in a chat happens only when you choose Send my location in a chat and confirm. Your phone then takes one reading and sends its position (rounded to 6 decimal places, about 10 cm) and how accurate it is, as a message to the people in that chat. They see it on a map, with a way to walk there, for one hour; then Skeeto's server deletes the position from the message (within a minute), leaving "Shared a location". It is not updated and it doesn't follow you. It doesn't need Precise Location: with that off, the position sent is the approximate one your phone gives Skeeto. Deleting the message deletes the position at once, and so does removing or blocking the person, for a one-to-one chat. If the message is reported, only "Shared a location" is kept for review, never the position.

On iPhone the map is drawn by Apple Maps, which loads map images from Apple under Apple's privacy policy. On Android it is drawn by Google Maps, which loads map images from Google under Google's privacy policy; Google sees which part of the map is shown and your IP address. Opening a walk in Apple Maps or Google Maps sends that app the destination only.

U of T sign-in

  • Your UTORid password never reaches Skeeto. You sign in on U of T's own login page, which is shown inside the app and served by U of T over an encrypted connection. The app doesn't read what you type there, and it doesn't store or send your password.
  • After you sign in, the app keeps the session cookies on your phone, the same way a browser does. They are sent only to U of T.
  • The app uses that session to read your timetable, program and year from ACORN. It does this on your phone, through ACORN's own pages.
  • To connect Quercus, it creates a Quercus access token named "Skeeto app" on your U of T account. The token is stored in your phone's secure storage (the Keychain on iPhone, encrypted with the Android Keystore on Android) and sent only to Quercus (q.utoronto.ca). If you paste in a Quercus calendar feed link instead, that is also kept in that secure storage.
  • Your timetable is read from ACORN and kept on your phone. Once a day, on its own, your phone also looks up your course codes in U of T's public course timetable, and when a class time there has moved in a way it can match one for one, it updates that time on your phone (never the room, which the public timetable does not list). It also looks them up when you open a class. These lookups don't identify you.
  • UTM's exam schedule. At Mississauga, from six weeks before an exam period until it ends, your phone opens UTM's final exam schedule page once a day with your U of T session, the same way you would in a browser, and adds an exam for each of your courses to your Exams list, marked "From UTM's schedule". If you already typed that exam in, what you typed stays and only the blanks are filled. If the page needs you to sign in again, it does nothing until another day. If the schedule later moves one of those exams, or splits its room so you need to pick yours, your phone updates it and, if exam reminders are on, shows one notification. Your exam list stays on your phone.

Skeeto doesn't send anything about you to U of T. Your phone talks to U of T's services directly, as you would in a browser. U of T sees that activity under its own policies.

3. What stays only on your phone

These things never reach Skeeto's server, with or without an account:

  • your UTORid password (the app never has it), your U of T session cookies, and your Quercus access token or calendar feed link;
  • your full timetable for every term (only this term's courses, sections, program and year are uploaded, and only with an account);
  • assignments, due dates, grades, submission status and course weights from Quercus, except a grade you choose to send in a chat (the course, "Current grade" and the score), which is stored as that message;
  • your exams: the list you see under Exams, with rooms, seats and notes, whether you typed them or UTM's schedule filled them (only the exam times above are shared, and not as yours);
  • habits, your parked car and EV charging timer (Mississauga only), groups you keep only for comparing weeks (a list of usernames kept only on your phone; group chats are stored on the server, as described above), and your appearance settings;
  • the sample week, if you try it: a made-up timetable, assignments and exam that exist only on your phone and are never uploaded, even with an account;
  • reminders (classes, assignments, exams, habits, parking), which are scheduled on your phone; during the break, class reminders for next term's first week are set ahead. 4. How it is used

Skeeto uses your information only to run Skeeto:

  • To provide the app: your account and profile, posts, comments, messages, Friends and shared timetables, communities and search.
  • To personalise Spotted. Posts are ranked using Spotted activity, who you follow, the communities you're in, your friends' reposts, and your courses and program. Your courses are turned into a "course profile" using U of T's public course descriptions. Your activity is combined into a "taste profile". Posts are also given a profile of their own from their text and images. Posts that match your profiles rank higher. How much you use Spotted, and the community you use most, decide whether Today shows a Spotted post, which one, and which Spotted notifications you get.
  • To suggest people you may know, from the classes, friends, program, year, communities and follows you share, as described above, unless you switch it off.
  • To improve the recommendations. Your Spotted activity may be used to train Skeeto's recommendation model, which learns what kinds of posts people engage with. The model stores learned patterns, not copies of anyone's records. Training only ever uses records that still exist.
  • To keep Spotted safe: the automated checks and human review described in the next section, enforcing the terms, and stopping people who were banned from coming back under a new account.
  • To understand which features are used, and to fix crashes, through the anonymous analytics and crash reports described above.
  • To fix food information: food corrections you send are read by the developer, who fixes the published hours if they are wrong. A correction never changes anything by itself.
  • To reply to you when you write to us.

Your courses, program and year aren't optional. If you have an account, they personalise Spotted, add you to your course communities, and show on your profile. There is no switch to turn this off. The Account tab shows what is used, under Courses & program. If you don't want your courses used this way, you can use Skeeto without an account, or delete your account. Your timetable, assignments and reminders work the same either way. You can leave any course community, and you won't be added back to it unless a later import finds that course as new.

Your information isn't sold, rented or traded. It isn't used for advertising, and it isn't used for any purpose not listed here unless you agree to that first.

5. Automated moderation and human review

Skeeto checks content automatically before and after it is posted, and a person reviews what is reported.

  • A list of blocked words is checked the moment you post or save posts, comments, links, messages, group names, profile fields, and community names, descriptions and rules. A match is refused.
  • A text model reads posts, comments, profile text (username, display name, bio, program and social handles) and community names, descriptions and rules. It runs in Skeeto's server programs (Supabase); the text isn't sent to any AI or moderation company. Text it rates as highly sexually explicit or severely toxic is removed. For a profile, the username and display name are replaced with a neutral one, and the bio, program and handles are cleared. Text checks never give a strike, except on an anonymous post or reply: one the model rates as a threat, an insult or an attack on who someone is (0.8 or more on its scale) is removed and gives its writer a strike.
  • Replies in an anonymous community are held for the text check. A reply there is shown only to you for the few seconds the check takes. If the check hasn't answered after about two minutes, the reply is shown anyway. The server notes that it went out unchecked, and deletes that note after 90 days.
  • Every image (in posts, comments and messages, community icons and profile photos) is checked by an image model before anyone else can see it. An image the first check is unsure about is held. Only you can see it until a second, stronger model decides. If that model rates the image as clearly explicit, the image is removed and your account gets a strike. If it rates it as somewhat explicit, the image is removed with no strike.
  • Support lines. When the words of a post or reply read like someone may be thinking of hurting themselves (a short list of phrases, checked on your phone as you type and again on the server), the app shows UTM's 24/7 student support lines: to the writer before it is sent, and at the top of that post's comments for everyone who reads them. Nothing is stopped, hidden or counted as a strike, and no analytics are recorded about it. The server marks the post or reply for the developer to look at, and emails him its text (up to 500 characters), where it was posted and whether it was anonymous, through Resend, without the writer's name. Nobody else can see that mark, the writer included, and it tells no one but the developer who wrote an anonymous post.
  • Strikes: a strike closes Spotted to you for 7 days. In that time you also can't send messages, like, start a community, or change your profile. A third strike ends posting, commenting, messaging and profile changes permanently.

Automated decisions. A strike from the image check, or from the text check on an anonymous post or reply, is made automatically, without a person looking first. So are your Spotted usage level, which decides Today's Spotted card and Spotted notifications, and who is suggested to you as people you may know. You can ask for any automated decision to be reviewed by a person, and tell us why you think it was wrong, by writing to lucasnadon3132@gmail.com. The developer can restore removed content and reverse strikes.

Reports. You can report any post, comment, profile or community, and you can report a person from a chat. Reports aren't anonymous to the developer, and the person you report isn't told who reported them. If three different people report the same post, comment or student-created community, it is hidden until it's reviewed. In an anonymous community, two reports are enough to hide a post or reply. Each report, each block and each strike is emailed to the developer through Resend, an email delivery service (block emails at most 5 an hour about one person and 30 an hour in all). A report email includes both usernames, the reason given, and up to 500 characters of the reported text. It never includes an image. A block email says who blocked whom (including a block made from an anonymous post or reply), how many people have blocked that account, and how many open reports it has. The person blocked isn't told.

Food corrections are emailed to the developer the same way, through Resend, without your name: the place, the reason, your words (up to 500 characters), what the app showed and when the hours were last checked, and how many corrections your account sent that day.

Human review. The developer reviews reports and can remove content, give or reverse strikes, and ban accounts. To do this he can look at reported, held and removed content, including images. Community creators can remove posts, comments and members in the communities they created. In an anonymous community they can also remove the person behind a post or reply, who then can't rejoin, post or reply there; Skeeto doesn't tell them who it was. The text model doesn't read messages, but the blocked-word list and the image check do. You can report or block the person you're talking to at any time.

Blocking hides the other person from you everywhere, and you from them: Spotted, comments, reposts, search, profiles and messages. It also ends any connection, and any follow either way, between you. Anonymous posts and replies they wrote before you blocked them stay, and so do ones written while the block was on after you unblock them, so that blocking can't be used to find out who wrote something.

Blocking from an anonymous post or reply hides that person's anonymous posts and replies from you everywhere, without telling you who they are. Their named posts and replies stay, and nothing else changes between you, because either would tell you who it was. They aren't told.

Some things are kept after deletion, for moderation and safety only:

  • If you had strikes or a ban, a record of them is kept. It is stored with a one-way scrambled (hashed) form of your email address and of your Apple or Google sign-in ID, so that signing up again with the same email or the same Apple or Google account brings the strikes and ban back. The hashes can't be turned back into your email address. There's no end date for this record.
  • Content that was removed, hidden by reports, or under an open report when you deleted your account is copied for review. That includes its text, link and images, and, for a reported profile, its last version. Images still waiting for the second image check are also kept. These copies aren't shown to anyone. There's no fixed date yet for deleting them.
  • Reports you made are kept without your name on them. Reports about you are kept without being linked to your account.

If the app says deletion isn't available, nothing has been deleted. Email lucasnadon3132@gmail.com from the address your account uses, or include your username, and the developer will delete the same things by hand.

Your timetable, assignments and other on-phone data stay on your phone until you delete the app. On iPhone, some Keychain items can survive deleting and reinstalling the app. On Android, your phone's Google backup can bring back scheduled reminders when you reinstall or move to a new phone; the Quercus token is never in that backup.

Two things are outside Skeeto, and you remove them yourself:

  • The Quercus access token. It's registered on your U of T account. Remove it in Quercus at Account → Settings → Approved Integrations, where it's called "Skeeto app" (or "Oakhall app" if you connected before September 2026).
  • Friends data from Oakhall, before September 2026 (only if you used Oakhall on an iPhone). Oakhall stored Friends in Apple's iCloud (CloudKit). Your copy is in your own iCloud account. Remove it in Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage. To have anything left in the shared database deleted, email us.

10. Security

  • All connections between the app and Skeeto's server, U of T, and the services above are encrypted.
  • The database enforces who can read what, row by row. The app can't read anything the rules don't allow, even if the app itself had a bug.
  • Photos are kept in private storage. They're only served to someone signed in who is allowed to see the post, comment or community they belong to.
  • Your Quercus token is kept in your phone's secure storage: the Keychain on iPhone, or encrypted with the Android Keystore on Android, where it is left out of phone backups. Server keys never ship in the app. The rented computers on Modal sign in to the database with a restricted account that can only reach what they need.

No system is perfectly secure. Remember that messages aren't end-to-end encrypted. If a breach puts your information at real risk of serious harm, we'll tell you, and the regulators, as the law requires.
