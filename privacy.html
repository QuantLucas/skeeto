Skeeto, Privacy Policy

Last updated 14 October 2026

Your timetable, assignments, grades and reminders stay on your phone, and they work without an account. A Skeeto account is optional. You need one for the Feed, Friends and messages, and what it stores is kept on Skeeto's server in Canada. If you have an account, your courses, program and year are used to personalise your Feed, to add you to your course communities, and to show on your profile, and you can't switch this off. Your location stays on your phone unless you turn on sharing it, on campus only, with friends you pick. Some processing happens in the United States; the details are below. Skeeto doesn't sell your data, doesn't show ads, and doesn't track you across other apps or websites.

Skeeto is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by the University of Toronto. It is an independent app. When you ask it to, it signs in to U of T's own services (ACORN and Quercus) using your own UTORid, on U of T's own sign-in page. Until September 2026 it was called Oakhall. It is the same app from the same developer.

1. Who we are

Skeeto is made and run by Lucas Nadon, an individual developer in Canada (referred to below as "Skeeto", "we" or "the developer"). He is responsible for the personal information Skeeto handles, and he is the person to contact about it, at lucasnadon3132@gmail.com.

This policy covers the Skeeto app for iPhone and the server it uses. It is written to meet Canada's federal privacy law (PIPEDA) and, for people in Quebec, Quebec's private-sector privacy law (as amended by Law 25).

2. What Skeeto collects

If you never make an account, Skeeto's server stores nothing about you, apart from short-term technical logs. In that case, besides your own sign-in to U of T, only two kinds of thing leave your phone. One is anonymous usage analytics, which you can turn off (see Usage analytics). The other is requests for public information, such as campus map and food data from Skeeto's server, campus photos, and U of T's public course timetable. Those requests don't include anything about you.

If you do make an account, this is everything Skeeto's server stores about you.

Your account

  • How you sign in. Sign in with Apple, Google, or a six-digit code sent to your email. We store your email address (with Sign in with Apple this may be a private relay address) and the ID Apple or Google uses for you. If Apple or Google gives us your name, we store it as a suggestion for your display name.
  • A record that you accepted the posting terms, with the version and the time.

Your profile

  • Your username, display name, bio, social handles (Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, Discord) if you add them, a profile photo if you have one, your campus, and the date you joined.
  • Your program of study and year. These are filled in automatically from ACORN (see the next section).
  • How many people follow you (your Broskeetos), how many you follow, and how many posts you have.

Your timetable, courses, program and year

  • Your enrolment: this term's course codes and sections, your program and your year, as read from ACORN. It is uploaded only if you have an account. A new import replaces it.
  • Your course communities. You are added automatically to one community per course you take (for example MAT102H5). Your membership is a record on the server.
  • Your week, for Friends. When you open the Friends tab while signed in, your current term's timetable is uploaded in two copies. One has times only (when you're busy). The other has the full details: course codes, sections, rooms and times. Who can read each copy is explained below.

Posts, comments, messages and photos

  • Posts: text (up to 2,000 characters), up to four photos, an optional link, and the community you posted in.
  • Comments and replies, which can include one image.
  • Likes, reposts and stickers, and posts you mark "Not interested".
  • Communities you create: name, description and icon.
  • Messages you send to and receive from people you're connected with, one to one or in a group chat, when each one was sent, edited or deleted, and when each member last read the conversation (this is what shows "Seen"). For a group chat we also store its name, who is in it, who added them and who runs it. A message can include one photo. Everyone in a group chat can see its members and messages, except that people who have blocked each other don't see each other's messages.
  • Photos. Skeeto uses only the photos you choose or take for a post, comment, message, sticker, community icon or profile photo. A profile photo or a photo in a message, like every other image, is checked by the automated image moderation described below before anyone else can see it; until then only you see it (for a message, the whole message, its words included). It never reads the rest of your library. Each photo is resized and re-saved as a JPEG on your phone before it is uploaded.
  • Reports you make: what you reported and the reason you gave.

Message notifications

  • Your iPhone's push token. If you allow notifications, the app sends Skeeto's server the push token Apple gives Skeeto on your phone (an address for notifications, which says nothing else about you or the phone), linked to your account, with whether the app is a test build or the App Store one and when it was last registered. It is used only to deliver message notifications. Signing out on that phone removes it.
  • Chats you muted: which conversations you don't want notifications from. Nobody else can see this, and the other people in the chat aren't told.
  • What a notification contains. When someone messages you, Skeeto's server sends Apple's push notification service the notification for your phone: the sender's display name (and the group's name, in a group chat), the start of the message (up to about 120 characters, or "Sent a photo" for a photo), how many unread messages you have, and the conversation's ID so a tap opens it. Apple delivers it to your phone. Nothing is sent for a chat you muted, or from someone either of you has blocked.

Your connections

  • Who you follow, and who follows you.
  • Friend requests, connections, and who you have promoted to friend.
  • The communities you have joined, including course communities.
  • People you have blocked.

What you do in the Feed

To decide what to show you, Skeeto records what happens in your Feed, and links it to your account:

  • which posts were shown to you, where in the list they were, and what score the ranking gave them;
  • how long you spent on each post, and whether you opened the full post or its comments;
  • what you liked or unliked, commented on, reposted or un-reposted, shared, or marked "Not interested", and whose profiles you opened from a post;
  • where in the app each of these happened (the Feed, a community, a profile, search).

Feed search. What you type into search is sent to Skeeto's server so it can find matching posts and communities. It isn't stored or logged. It is used only to answer that search.

Usage analytics

Skeeto sends a small, fixed set of usage events to PostHog, an analytics service, so we can tell which features are actually used. This is on by default. You can turn it off at Account → Privacy → Help improve Skeeto. When it's off, nothing more is sent, and from the next time the app starts, the analytics library isn't started at all.

EventWhat is sent with it

You imported your timetableHow many courses were found, and that the source was ACORN. Not which courses

The app's code allows two more events, with no details attached: sending or accepting a friend request. The app doesn't currently send either of them.

  • Not linked to your account. Events carry a random ID that the app creates for analytics on your phone. It isn't your username, your account ID, or your UTORid, and we don't connect it to them.
  • Device details. The PostHog software adds standard details to each event: app version, iOS version, device type (phone or tablet), screen size, language and time zone. It doesn't send your device's name or any advertising identifier.
  • No location, ever. A filter in the app removes any location-like value from every event before it's sent, including events PostHog's own software creates. The app also tells PostHog not to work out your location from your IP address. PostHog's servers still see the IP address a request comes from, as every server does.
  • Never your Feed activity. What you do in the Feed is recorded only by Skeeto's own server, as described above. It never goes to PostHog.
  • There is no crash reporter, no session recording or screen replay, and no advertising or data-broker software of any kind.

Technical data

Like any online service, Skeeto's server providers keep short-term technical logs of the requests your phone makes, such as the IP address, the time and the kind of request. They are used for security and to fix problems. Skeeto's own server code logs IDs and outcomes, not the text or images you post.

Location

Your location is used on your phone, and only if you allow it:

  • to show where you are on the campus map;
  • to estimate how long the walk to your next class will take;
  • for walking directions you start yourself. While a walk is running, the app follows your location in the background, including when the screen is locked, so the directions on your Lock Screen stay up to date. iOS shows its location indicator the whole time. Following stops when you arrive or end the walk.

None of that is sent anywhere: not to Skeeto's server, not to analytics, not to other students.

Sharing where you are with friends is the one exception. It is on by default for your friends: the first time you open this version of Skeeto it tells you so, with a button to turn it off, and you can turn it off at any time in Account → Location. While it is on, every friend can see you (and each new friend once you both accept), except anyone you untick there. It only works if you allow Skeeto to use your location. When it is on:

  • Only on campus. Your phone sends your position only while you are on your own campus: Mississauga, St. George or Scarborough. St. George is spread through downtown streets, so there it sends only while you are close to a university building, not anywhere inside the campus outline. When you leave, it deletes it. Skeeto's server also refuses any position outside the three campus areas, and only friends at your own campus can see it.
  • When it updates. Your position is sent while the app is on your screen, about every few minutes or when you move. If you also allow location "Always", a friend you picked who looks for you can ask for a fresh position with Skeeto closed: Skeeto is woken for a moment, takes one reading and sends it (still only on campus), at most once every two minutes. It never follows you between asks. Without "Always", nothing is sent while Skeeto is closed.
  • What is stored: one current position (latitude and longitude, how accurate your phone says it is, the campus building it is nearest to, and the time), linked to your account on Skeeto's server. Each new position replaces the last one; no history of where you have been is kept.
  • Who sees it: only the friends you picked, as a face on the campus map and a line on your card naming the nearest campus building and how long ago it was updated. It stops at once if either of you removes or blocks the other, and becoming friends again doesn't turn it back on.
  • How long: nothing older than two hours is ever shown, and older positions are deleted automatically. Turning sharing off, unpicking everyone, leaving campus, signing out or deleting your account deletes your position straight away.

Like everything on the server, the developer can technically see a stored position. He doesn't look at them, and they are never used for analytics, the Feed or anything but showing your friends where you are.

The map is drawn by Apple Maps, which loads map images from Apple under Apple's own privacy policy.

U of T sign-in

  • Your UTORid password never reaches Skeeto. You sign in on U of T's own login page, which is shown inside the app and served by U of T over an encrypted connection. The app doesn't read what you type there, and it doesn't store or send your password.
  • After you sign in, the app keeps the session cookies on your phone, the same way a browser does. They are sent only to U of T.
  • The app uses that session to read your timetable, program and year from ACORN. It does this on your phone, through ACORN's own pages.
  • To connect Quercus, it creates a Quercus access token named "Skeeto app" on your U of T account. The token is stored in your iPhone's Keychain and sent only to Quercus (q.utoronto.ca). If you paste in a Quercus calendar feed link instead, that is also kept in the Keychain.
  • Your phone also looks up course codes in U of T's public course timetable. These lookups don't identify you.

Skeeto doesn't send anything about you to U of T. Your phone talks to U of T's services directly, as you would in a browser. U of T sees that activity under its own policies.

3. What stays only on your phone

These things never reach Skeeto's server, with or without an account:

  • your UTORid password (the app never has it), your U of T session cookies, and your Quercus access token or calendar feed link;
  • your full timetable for every term (only this term's courses, sections, program and year are uploaded, and only with an account);
  • assignments, due dates, grades, submission status and course weights from Quercus;
  • exams, habits, your parked car and EV charging timer (Mississauga only), the waitlist watcher, groups you keep only for comparing weeks (a list of usernames kept only on your phone; group chats are stored on the server, as described above), and your appearance settings;
  • the sample week, if you try it: a made-up timetable, assignments and exam that exist only on your phone and are never uploaded, even with an account;
  • reminders (classes, assignments, exams, habits, parking, the waitlist), which are scheduled on your phone. The only notifications that come from Skeeto's server are message notifications;
  • Lock Screen and Live Activity cards, and walking routes;
  • your location, unless you turn on sharing it with friends (see Location);
  • your analytics on/off setting and the random analytics ID.

4. How it is used

Skeeto uses your information only to run Skeeto:

  • To provide the app: your account and profile, posts, comments, messages, Friends and shared timetables, communities and search.
  • To personalise your Feed. Posts are ranked using your Feed activity, who you follow, the communities you're in, your friends' reposts, and your courses and program. Your courses are turned into a "course profile" using U of T's public course descriptions. Your activity is combined into a "taste profile". Posts are also given a profile of their own from their text and images. Posts that match your profiles rank higher.
  • To improve the recommendations. Your Feed activity may be used to train Skeeto's recommendation model, which learns what kinds of posts people engage with. The model stores learned patterns, not copies of anyone's records. Training only ever uses records that still exist.
  • To keep the Feed safe: the automated checks and human review described in the next section, enforcing the terms, and stopping people who were banned from coming back under a new account.
  • To understand which features are used, through the anonymous analytics described above.
  • To reply to you when you write to us.

Your courses, program and year aren't optional. If you have an account, they personalise your Feed, add you to your course communities, and show on your profile. There is no switch to turn this off. The Account tab shows what is used, under Courses & program. If you don't want your courses used this way, you can use Skeeto without an account, or delete your account. Your timetable, assignments and reminders work the same either way. You can leave any course community, and you won't be added back to it unless a later import finds that course as new.

Your information isn't sold, rented or traded. It isn't used for advertising, and it isn't used for any purpose not listed here unless you agree to that first.

5. Automated moderation and human review

Skeeto checks content automatically before and after it is posted, and a person reviews what is reported.

  • A list of blocked words is checked the moment you post or save posts, comments, links, profile fields, and community names and descriptions. A match is refused.
  • A text model reads posts, comments, profile text (username, display name, bio, program and social handles) and community names and descriptions. Text it rates as highly sexually explicit or severely toxic is removed. For a profile, the username and display name are replaced with a neutral one, and the bio, program and handles are cleared. Text checks never give a strike.
  • Every image (in posts, comments and messages, community icons and profile photos) is checked by an image model before anyone else can see it. An image the first check is unsure about is held. Only you can see it until a second, stronger model decides. If that model rates the image as clearly explicit, the image is removed and your account gets a strike. If it rates it as somewhat explicit, the image is removed with no strike.
  • Strikes: a strike closes the Feed to you for 7 days. A third strike ends posting and commenting permanently.

Automated decisions. A strike from the image check is made automatically, without a person looking first. You can ask for any automated decision to be reviewed by a person, and tell us why you think it was wrong, by writing to lucasnadon3132@gmail.com. The developer can restore removed content and reverse strikes.

Reports. You can report any post, comment, profile or community, and you can report a person from a chat. Reports aren't anonymous to the developer, and the person you report isn't told who reported them. If three different people report the same post, comment or student-created community, it is hidden until it's reviewed. Each report and each strike is emailed to the developer through Resend, an email delivery service. A report email includes both usernames, the reason given, and up to 500 characters of the reported text. It never includes an image.

Human review. The developer reviews reports and can remove content, give or reverse strikes, and ban accounts. To do this he can look at reported, held and removed content, including images. Community creators can remove posts, comments and members in the communities they created. Messages aren't scanned automatically, but you can report or block the person you're talking to at any time.

Blocking hides the other person from you everywhere, and you from them: the Feed, comments, reposts, search, profiles and messages. It also ends any connection, and any follow either way, between you.

Other disclosures

We may disclose information if the law requires it, or if we believe in good faith that disclosing it is needed to protect someone from serious harm. If Skeeto is ever transferred to someone else, your information would go with it only under this policy, and you would be told first.

Skeeto doesn't sell your information. It doesn't share it with advertisers or data brokers, doesn't show ads, and doesn't track you across other companies' apps or websites.

7. Where it is stored and processed

  • Skeeto's database and photo storage are in Canada, in Supabase's Montréal region.
  • Supabase's server programs run the automated text and image checks, search, report emails and account deletion. Supabase normally runs them in the data centre closest to the request. Account deletion is always run in Montréal. The others usually run in Canada, but Supabase doesn't guarantee it, so they may run in another region, such as the United States.
  • In the United States: held images and the Feed's profiles (Modal), analytics (PostHog), and report and strike emails (Resend). The developer's mailbox is Gmail.
  • Message notifications pass through Apple's push notification service, which may be outside Canada.

Information processed outside Canada is subject to the laws of that country, and may be accessible to its courts and authorities.

When you delete your account

In the app, go to Account → Delete account. This permanently deletes your account, profile, posts, comments, photos, reposts, likes, follows, stickers, "Not interested" choices, community memberships, messages (from both sides of each conversation), enrolment, shared timetables, connections, blocks, Feed activity, taste profile, terms record, push tokens and muted chats. Other people's comments on your posts, and replies under your comments, go with them. Communities you created stay up without you. Replies elsewhere that were addressed to you stay, but they're no longer linked to your account.

Some things are kept after deletion, for moderation and safety only:

  • If you had strikes or a ban, a record of them is kept. It is stored with a one-way scrambled (hashed) form of your email address and of your Apple or Google sign-in ID, so that signing up again with the same email or the same Apple or Google account brings the strikes and ban back. The hashes can't be turned back into your email address. There's no end date for this record.
  • Content that was removed, hidden by reports, or under an open report when you deleted your account is copied for review. That includes its text, link and images, and, for a reported profile, its last version. Images still waiting for the second image check are also kept. These copies aren't shown to anyone. There's no fixed date yet for deleting them.
  • Reports you made are kept without your name on them. Reports about you are kept without being linked to your account.

If the app says deletion isn't available, nothing has been deleted. Email lucasnadon3132@gmail.com from the address your account uses, or include your username, and the developer will delete the same things by hand.

Your timetable, assignments and other on-phone data stay on your phone until you delete the app. Some Keychain items can survive deleting and reinstalling the app.

Two things are outside Skeeto, and you remove them yourself:

  • The Quercus access token. It's registered on your U of T account. Remove it in Quercus at Account → Settings → Approved Integrations, where it's called "Skeeto app" (or "Oakhall app" if you connected before September 2026).
  • Friends data from Oakhall, before September 2026. Oakhall stored Friends in Apple's iCloud (CloudKit). Your copy is in your own iCloud account. Remove it in Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage. To have anything left in the shared database deleted, email us.

9. Your choices and rights

Choices in the app

  • Analytics: Account → Privacy → Help improve Skeeto.
  • Location, photos and camera: allow or refuse them in iOS Settings → Skeeto. Everything else in the app works without them.
  • Message notifications: mute any chat from its … menu, or turn notifications off in iOS Settings → Skeeto → Notifications.
  • Your profile: edit or clear your display name, bio and social handles at any time. Your program and year follow your ACORN import.
  • Your week: connections see only your busy times. You choose who you promote to friend, and only they see your full timetable. Reducing someone back to connection, or removing them, takes effect immediately. There's no separate switch yet to take your shared week off the server. Deleting your account removes it.
  • Where you are: sharing your location with friends is on by default; turn it off, or untick particular friends, in Account → Location. Turning it off deletes your position from the server at once and stops new friends seeing it.
  • Course communities: leave any of them to come off their member lists.
  • Your content: delete your posts, comments and messages, unfollow, leave communities, and block people.
  • Your account: delete it in the app, as described above.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • ask what personal information Skeeto holds about you, and get a copy of it;
  • ask for it to be corrected if it's wrong;
  • have it deleted;
  • withdraw your consent to a use, though some uses are part of having an account, so withdrawing means deleting the account;
  • ask for an automated decision to be reviewed by a person;
  • in Quebec, ask for your information in a structured, commonly used format.

There's no in-app export yet, so email lucasnadon3132@gmail.com for any of these. We may need to confirm that the account is yours. We'll answer within 30 days.

If you aren't satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. In Quebec, you can complain to the Commission d'accès à l'information. You can also complain to your own province's privacy regulator.

10. Security

  • All connections between the app and Skeeto's server, U of T, and the services above are encrypted.
  • The database enforces who can read what, row by row. The app can't read anything the rules don't allow, even if the app itself had a bug.
  • Photos are kept in private storage. They're only served to someone signed in who is allowed to see the post, comment or community they belong to.
  • Your Quercus token is kept in the iPhone Keychain. Server keys never ship in the app. The rented computers on Modal sign in to the database with a restricted account that can only reach what they need.

No system is perfectly secure. Remember that messages aren't end-to-end encrypted. If a breach puts your information at real risk of serious harm, we'll tell you, and the regulators, as the law requires.

11. Children

Skeeto is made for university students. It isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect information from children under 13. You must be at least 13 to create a Skeeto account. If you live in Quebec and are under 14, you need a parent's or guardian's consent. The app doesn't ask your age or check it. If you believe a child under 13 (or a child under 14 in Quebec, without consent) has an account, email us and we'll delete it.

The timetable, assignments, campus map and food information work without an account, at any age.

12. Changes to this policy

If we change what Skeeto collects, why, or where it goes, we'll update this page and the date at the top before the change takes effect. We'll describe the change in the app's release notes. If a change needs your consent, such as a new use of information already collected, we'll ask in the app first.
