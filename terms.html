Skeeto, Terms of Use

Last updated 20 November 2026 · Posting rules version ugc-2026-11-20

Skeeto is for students, and it has no tolerance for objectionable content or abusive users. These terms are the agreement between you and Skeeto, operated by Lucas Nadon, for using the app.

How your information is handled is in the privacy policy. Help is on the support page.

1. Agreeing to these terms

By using Skeeto, you agree to these terms. When you create a Skeeto account, the app shows you the posting rules in section 3 and asks you to accept them; you can't make an account without accepting. If the rules change in meaning, the app asks again before you next use Spotted or post. If you don't agree, don't make an account. You can still use the parts of Skeeto that don't need one.

2. Who can use Skeeto

  • You must be at least 13 to create a Skeeto account. If you live in Quebec and are under 14, you need a parent's or guardian's consent. If you're under the age of majority where you live, a parent or guardian should read these terms with you.
  • One account per person. Don't use another person's account, and don't pretend to be someone else.
  • Keep access to your account to yourself. You're responsible for what happens on it.
  • If you've been banned from Skeeto, don't create another account.
  • You confirm that you're not in a country subject to a Canadian or U.S. government embargo, and not on a Canadian or U.S. government list of prohibited or restricted parties.
  • You must also follow the terms of any other service you use with Skeeto, such as your wireless plan.

3. The posting rules

These are the rules you accept in the app before you post or message, exactly as the app shows them.

Before you post or message

Skeeto is for students, and it has no tolerance for objectionable content or abusive users.

Not allowed

  • Nudity or sexual content of any kind.
  • Harassment, bullying, threats, hate, or content that encourages violence or self-harm.
  • Sharing someone's private information or photos without their permission, or pretending to be someone else.
  • Spam, scams, questions or answers from an exam, and anything illegal.
  • These rules cover everything you put on Skeeto: posts, comments, images, your profile, communities, messages and group chats.

What happens

  • Anything that breaks these rules is removed, and so are accounts that abuse other people.
  • Every image is checked automatically. One the first check is unsure about stays visible only to you until a second check decides.
  • A strike is an image confirmed as explicit, or a threat, insult or attack in an anonymous post or reply. It is removed, and for 7 days you can't use Spotted or send messages.
  • Third strike and you can never post, comment or send messages again.

Reporting and blocking

  • Report any post, comment, profile or community, or someone in a chat, and it goes straight to Skeeto. Enough reports hide a post, comment or community until it is reviewed.
  • Block someone and they disappear for you everywhere, messages included, and you for them.

Anonymous communities

  • Nobody sees who wrote a post there, but Skeeto keeps it. Each one has rules you accept first: no naming students, no contact details. Two reports hide a post there.

Your courses and program

  • Your courses, program and year from ACORN tune Spotted and put you in your course communities, which you can leave. Your program and year appear on your profile.

Who can join

  • You must be at least 13. You are responsible for what you post and send.
  • Questions, appeals, or something we missed: lucasnadon3132@gmail.com.

The rules apply to everything you put on Skeeto: posts, comments, images, links, stickers, messages and group chats (including a group's name), cards you send in a chat (a post, a location, a grade or a food run), your profile (username, display name, bio, social handles and photo), and the communities you create, including any rules you add to one.

4. How the rules are enforced

  • Automatic checks. Words that are never allowed are refused when you post, comment, send a message or change your profile. Text and images are checked automatically, and content that clearly breaks the rules is removed. A profile whose text breaks the rules is reset to a neutral username, with its bio, program and handles cleared. The privacy policy explains how the checks work.
  • Strikes. You get a strike when the second image check confirms an image is explicit. You also get one when the text check finds a threat, an insult or an attack on who someone is in an anonymous post or reply. A strike removes it. For 7 days you can't open Spotted (its communities, posts and search), post, comment, like, send messages, start a community, or change your name, bio, handles or photo. With a third strike, you can never post, comment, send messages or change your profile again. For 30 days after a strike, Skeeto doesn't suggest you to other students.
  • Removal and bans. Anything that breaks the rules is removed, whether it's found automatically, through reports, or by the developer. Accounts that abuse other people are banned from posting, and they may be removed from Skeeto entirely.
  • Strikes and bans follow the person, not the account. If you delete your account and sign up again with the same email address, or the same Apple or Google account, your strikes and any ban come back.
  • Community creators can remove posts, comments and members in the communities they create. In an anonymous community they can remove the person who wrote a post or reply without learning who it was; that person can't rejoin or post there again. Creators must follow these rules too.
  • Appeals. If you think a removal or strike was wrong, including one made automatically, email lucasnadon3132@gmail.com. A person will look at it. Strikes made in error are reversed, and wrongly removed content is restored.

5. Anonymous communities

  • You can start a community as anonymous. You choose when you make it, and it can't be changed later.
  • In one, every post and reply is anonymous. Other students see "Anonymous", or a two-word name for you in that one thread. You can't post anonymously anywhere else, except in reply to a post that is itself anonymous (posts from before November 2026, and the threads Skeeto opens after an exam).
  • Before your first post or reply in one, you accept its rules: Skeeto's rules for anonymous communities, plus anything its creator added. Those include: don't name or describe a real student, don't post rumours or claims about a named person, and don't post threats, slurs or attacks on who someone is. If the rules change, you accept them again.
  • Anonymous posts and replies can't have photos or stickers. One with a phone number, an email address or an @handle is refused. Don't try to get contact details past that check in another form.
  • A reply there shows only to you until the text check reads it, for up to a few minutes.
  • A threat, insult or attack on who someone is in any anonymous post or reply is removed, and it is a strike.
  • Two reports from different people hide a post or reply there until it's reviewed.
  • You can write up to 5 anonymous posts a day, and up to 10 anonymous replies in 10 minutes or 30 in a day.
  • Nobody can see who is in an anonymous community, its creator included.
  • Skeeto keeps who wrote every anonymous post and reply. Other students and the community's creator never see it. The developer can look it up to deal with a report, a strike or a safety concern, and may disclose it where the law requires it or to protect someone from serious harm, as the privacy policy says.
  • Don't try to work out or reveal who wrote an anonymous post.

6. Reporting and blocking

  • Report any post, comment, profile or community from its menu. In a chat, report the person from the top of the conversation. The person you report isn't told who reported them.
  • Every report is sent to the developer. If three different people report the same post, comment or student-created community, it's hidden at once until it's reviewed. In an anonymous community, two reports are enough to hide a post or reply. Skeeto aims to review reports within 24 hours. Objectionable content is removed, and so are accounts that abuse other people.
  • Block someone, and they disappear for you everywhere, and you for them: Spotted, comments, reposts, search, profiles and messages. Blocking also ends any connection or follow between you. Anonymous posts and replies they wrote before you blocked them stay, so a block can't show who wrote something.
  • From an anonymous post or reply, you can block its writer without learning who they are. That hides their anonymous posts and replies from you. They aren't told.
  • If someone is in danger, don't wait for a report. Call 911.

7. Your content

  • You own what you post. You're responsible for it, and you must have the right to share it, including any photo of another person.
  • To run Skeeto, you give Skeeto a non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to store, copy, resize, display and send your content to the people you shared it with. This includes analysing it for moderation, search and recommendations: checking it automatically, turning it into the numerical profiles Spotted ranks with, and using it to improve the recommendation model. The licence lasts only as long as needed to run Skeeto. It ends when you delete the content or your account, except for the copies the privacy policy says are kept for moderation and safety.
  • Messages are only between you and the people in the conversation (one person, or the members of a group chat), but they aren't end-to-end encrypted. Anyone in a conversation can report a message to us, and a reported message is shared with the developer so it can be reviewed.
  • You can send a post, your location, a grade or a food run as a card in a chat. A shared post shows only to people who are allowed to see it, and an anonymous post stays anonymous. A shared location lasts 60 minutes, then Skeeto deletes the point; it ends sooner if you stop it or, in a one-to-one chat, if you remove or block the person. A grade card shows the course, what it's for and the score to everyone in that chat. Only share your own grades.
  • Posts in communities go out together at 10pm, Toronto time. Until then only you can see them. Posts to Friends and replies go out at once (a reply in an anonymous community after its text check).
  • After a final exam, Skeeto may start an anonymous thread in the course's community. Replies to it close after 48 hours.
  • A residence community is for the people who live there. Don't share its link with people who don't.

8. Using Skeeto fairly

Don't:

  • try to get around a mute, strike or ban, including by making a new account;
  • use bots, scripts or automated accounts, or collect other students' information from Skeeto (scraping);
  • try to read or change data you aren't allowed to, or test, overload or attack Skeeto's servers;
  • use Skeeto to advertise, sell, or recruit people for commercial purposes without permission;
  • share questions or answers from an exam, in an exam thread or anywhere on Skeeto. Some students write it later;
  • use Skeeto in a way that breaks the law, or U of T's rules for ACORN and Quercus.

Skeeto limits how much you can send in a short time, so scripts can't flood it. For example: 10 posts in 10 minutes or 60 a day, 30 comments in 10 minutes or 300 a day, 5 new communities a day, 30 friend requests a day, 60 messages in 10 minutes or 600 a day, and 10 food corrections a day. If you reach a limit, the app tells you, and for most limits when you can try again.
